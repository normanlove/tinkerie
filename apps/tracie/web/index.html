Serial in 10 seconds

  1. Plug in your Arduino / ESP32 / Pico. Its COM port appears in the list with the USB chip it uses.
  2. Pick the baud rate your sketch uses (Serial.begin(115200) → 115200) and press Connect.
  3. Print numbers like Serial.printf("temp:%.1f,hum:%.1f\n", t, h) and they're plotted live.

Pico & Arduino Leonardo need DTR on to send data. ESP32 boards reset through DTR/RTS — use Reset board. Native-USB boards that re-enumerate on reset reconnect automatically.

Logic analyser — one-time setup

Works with the cheap 24 MHz 8-channel "Saleae-compatible" analysers (Cypress FX2, shows up as VID 0925 / PID 3881) using the open-source fx2lafw firmware from the sigrok project.

  1. Driver: download Zadig from zadig.akeo.ie, plug in the analyser, choose Options → List All Devices, pick the analyser (often "Unknown device #1"), select WinUSB and press Install/Replace Driver.
  2. Firmware: if PulseView is installed, Tracie finds the firmware automatically. Otherwise grab fx2lafw-saleae-logic.fw (sigrok-firmware-fx2lafw package) and drop it on the Firmware needed box.
  3. Press Capture. The first capture loads the firmware into the analyser (takes a second).

Inputs are 3.3 V/5 V tolerant — never connect them to anything higher. Always connect GND. Use a sample rate at least 4× your fastest signal (10× for clean timing). 24 MHz may drop data on busy USB hubs; plug straight into the PC.

Shared timeline

Serial data and logic captures are timestamped against the same PC clock, so serial lines appear on the logic view (Serial overlay) and everything lines up on the Timeline tab. Alignment is within a few milliseconds (USB latency) — great for "what was the board printing when that I²C glitch happened?".
