Fixie

Version 0.4.1-alpha · in Tinkerie 0.30.1-alphaAlpha

Find the fault, down to the part. Keep it out of e-waste.

In plain words

The TV has sound but no picture, the controller won't charge, the speaker went quiet. Say what it is, how it's powered and what it has, then what it's doing, and Fixie takes you through it one check at a time, easiest first: what to do, which tools to use, what a good and a bad result look like. Type in your meter reading and it tells you which it is. Each answer leads to the next check, until it reaches the likely fault, how hard it is to fix and the parts you'll need. It only asks you to open things up when it has to, and tells you how to do it safely.

Everything it does

Features

Board level

Find the failed part, and keep old tech going.

  • Thirteen sections of a board: start from the beginning, or go to the power input, mains power supply, regulators and rails, battery and charging, clock and reset, processor and memory, data buses, audio, video, outputs, inputs, or wiring
  • Component-level steps: diode-test the rectifier, find the shorted capacitor by injecting current, check the crystal is oscillating, read the boot messages, decode the I2C bus to see which chip doesn't answer, follow a test tone to where it stops
  • Mains power supplies: cold tests first, then a dim bulb tester; startup resistors, output diodes and feedback
  • Salvage notes: what's worth saving from a board that can't be revived

My build

Troubleshooting your own projects.

  • Power, current draw and regulator pinouts; wiring against your design (opens Wirie); code that won't run or keeps resetting
  • I2C, SPI and serial parts that don't answer: addresses, pull-ups, levels, baud rates
  • Signals, noise and decoupling, motors, servos and steppers, addressable LEDs

Works with Tinkerie

Your instruments, in the steps.

  • Each step says which app to use and how to set it up, and opens it for you: Meterie, Scopie, Probie, Flashie or Wirie
  • Fill a reading straight from Meterie's meter, a Scopie measurement (frequency, peak-to-peak, minimum…) or a Probie logic channel (frequency, duty cycle), converted to the step's units and judged

Pictures and reference

See what you're looking for.

  • 55 drawings: what each part looks like with its pins labelled, where the meter probes, scope probe or logic analyser clips go, and what a good and a faulty signal look like on the screen
  • Waveforms for crystals, switching regulators, I2C (ACK and NAK, weak pull-ups), serial, reset, composite video and VGA sync, PWM, servo pulses, brownouts, noise and clipping
  • Reference cards: resistor colour code and SMD resistor (including EIA-96) and capacitor marking decoders, common pinouts, what board labels like R, C, Q, U and Y mean, finding pin 1, rail voltages, and Ohm's law, LED resistor and regulator heat calculators

Parts tests

How to test each kind of part.

  • 25 parts: resistors, pots, capacitors, diodes, zeners, LEDs, bridge rectifiers, transistors, MOSFETs, regulators, buck converters, crystals, logic chips, microcontrollers, op-amps, optocouplers, fuses, protection parts, relays, switches, connectors, speakers, motors, lithium cells and displays
  • Out of the circuit, in it and powered; what to replace it with; whether it's worth saving from scrap

Step by step

From symptom to fault.

  • Easy checks first: the socket, the charger, the batteries, the lead
  • Each step says what to do, why, which tools to use, and what a good and a bad result look like
  • Steps that don't apply to your device are skipped (no mains checks on a phone, no picture checks without a screen)
  • Type in your meter reading: Fixie compares it with what it should be and highlights the matching answer
  • Works out what a heating element should read from its voltage and power
  • Go back to any step and choose differently

What it covers

Almost anything electronic.

  • No list of products: you describe it by how it's powered (mains, adapter, USB, batteries, built-in battery) and what it has (screen, sound, buttons, touch, wireless, USB, motor, fan, LEDs, a programmable board…)
  • Categories such as Computer, Phone, Console, TV, Audio, Toy, Cordless tool, Lights, Electronics project and Household gadget fill in the usual answers
  • Dead, cuts out, won't charge, no picture, no sound, buttons, motors, heating, USB and wireless, uploads, LEDs, overheating, water damage, faults that come and go, tripping the electricity

Safety first

It tells you when to stop.

  • A safety check before every step inside something that runs from the mains or has a lithium battery
  • A warning before any measurement with the power on
  • Refuses outright, with no repair steps, anything dangerous: microwaves, ovens, toasters, kettles, heaters and anything else that heats from the mains, large appliances, high-voltage devices (CRTs, camera flashes), house wiring and gas, big batteries (e-bikes, scooters, cars) and medical or life-safety devices
  • A Safety tab on mains, big capacitors, lithium batteries, soldering and static
  • Each fix says how hard it is, and when a repair shop is the better choice

Tools and signs

Know what you're looking at.

  • 26 tools, from a multimeter to a hot air station: what each is for, roughly what it costs, how to use it
  • Tick the tools you have; each step marks them
  • Pictures of good and failed parts: bulging capacitors, burnt parts, corrosion, cracked joints, blown fuses, swollen batteries, damaged ports

Your repairs

Kept as you go.

  • Every repair is saved with each step, answer, reading and note
  • Carry on where you left off, even after closing Fixie
  • Save a report to keep, or to take to a repair shop
Before you start

Good to know

Fixie is a guide, not a guarantee. If a step feels unsafe, stop: a repair shop can take it from where you got to, and your report shows them what you've checked.
